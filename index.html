AUTOMATION PLATFORMSJ DESIGN STUDIO × SAMSUNG

SJ Design Automation Hub

The problem

Publishing localized Samsung FAQ content meant coordinating an Excel tracker, Jira, Adobe Experience Manager (AEM), and many country sites. Each article can require work across 89 locales, and multiple FAQ articles move through the process week to week. For every locale, someone had to find the right ticket, advance it through workflow stages, check that the page was live, update the tracker, review localized content, and sometimes copy approved work to related sites. The work was repetitive, hard to monitor at scale, and easy to leave half-finished.

What I built

A local web application that brings those workflows into one guided interface using the user’s existing Samsung SSO session. It processes pending locales from a workbook, finds matching Jira tickets using locale and URL-slug data, advances workflow stages, publishes and verifies pages, and writes confirmed live URLs back immediately. Separate automations audit FAQs against a baseline—with human approval retained for editorial decisions—and copy approved AEM components to child sites with duplicate protection and targeted retries.

Operational impact

The hub replaces a week-to-week chain of manual searching, status checking, tab switching, and tracker maintenance with a visible, repeatable run. It preserves completed work, verifies results before recording them, and makes exceptions actionable—freeing the content team from coordination overhead while leaving editorial judgment with the people responsible for it.

≈80% less workflow time · dozens of hours saved monthly
AI PRODUCTLECTERN

Lectern — AI Lecture Notes

The problem

Students have to choose between listening closely and capturing detailed notes. Recording a lecture preserves the material, but leaves the difficult work of transcribing, organizing, and turning it into something useful afterward. That work steals time from learning and still leaves students responsible for deciding what matters.

What I built

Lectern is a public AI note-taking product that turns recorded lectures into structured notes. I built the end-to-end experience around audio ingestion, transcription, note generation, application logic, user workflows, and production deployment—taking it from an idea to a product designed for real users.

Operational impact

Lectern moves the mechanical work of converting a recording into usable notes out of the student’s workflow. Students can stay present in the lecture, then return to organized material rather than an unstructured recording or a rushed set of partial notes.

AGENT ENGINEERINGDOBOTAI

Production Agents & Developer Education

The problem

Teams do not need another isolated chatbot; they need systems that can work within the tools, information, and handoffs that already make up the job. In content production, status and decisions are often scattered across people, Slack threads, and tracking systems—creating continuous coordination work. Developers new to agents also need to understand the architecture beneath the tooling before they can build with it confidently.

What I built

As Head of Agentic Operations at DobotAI, I built custom client agents and a curriculum that teaches beginners to build ReAct-style agents from first principles: reasoning loops, tool use, state, execution, and practical AI-assisted development with Claude Code.

Operational impact

For a content company, I built a project-management agent that worked across Slack and Airtable. It could communicate with team members, retrieve and update production status, track videos through the workflow, and coordinate the people and information involved in getting work to completion—reducing the manual follow-up required to keep production moving.
